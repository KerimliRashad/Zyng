## 🇺🇸 United States

### Legal Height Limits

**Federal Standard**
- **Maximum**: 13 feet 6 inches = **4.29 meters**
- **Minimum**: 13 feet = **4.11 meters**
- **Federal limit**: 4.11m to 4.29m

### State Variations

| State | Max Height | Notes |
|-------|-----------|-------|
| Most states | 4.29m | Federal standard |
| Some western | 4.36m | Special permits |
| Special permits | Up to 4.5m | Requires escort |

### Common US Truck Heights

- **Standard Semi-Trailer**: 4.01m - 4.19m ✅
- **Box Truck (53ft)**: 4.06m ✅
- **Refrigerated Truck**: 4.11m - 4.19m ✅
- **Flatbed**: Varies, usually 4m ✅
- **Oversized loads**: 4.3m+ ⚠️ (needs permit)

### Enforcement

- ❌ Violating height restriction = hefty fines
- 📸 Many bridges have height sensors
- 🚨 State police monitor major highways
- 💰 Fines: $100 - $500+ per violation

### Tips for US Routes

1. **Clearance Zones**
   - Old downtown areas (historic buildings)
   - Tunnels through mountains
   - Covered bridges
   - Overpass underpasses

2. **Safe Routing**
   - Avoid I-76 & certain PA interstate bridges
   - Check Local height restrictions
   - Use truck-friendly routes (I-80, I-90)
   - Avoid city centers with 4m clearance

3. **Planning**
   - Add 30cm safety margin
   - Always set max to 3.96m for safety
   - Know your exact truck height
   - Use GPS with height aware routing

## 🇪🇺 European Union

### Legal Height Limits

**EU Standard (Directive 96/53/EC)**
- **Maximum**: 4.30 meters (14 feet 1 inch)
- **Minimum**: 4.00 meters (13 feet 1 inch)
- **Standard**: 4.00m to 4.30m

### Country Variations

| Country | Max Height | Notes |
|---------|-----------|-------|
| Germany | 4.30m | Strict enforcement |
| France | 4.30m | Tolls + enforcement |
| UK | 4.30m | Brexit borders |
| Poland | 4.30m | Main routes safe |
| Spain | 4.30m | Mountain passes |
| Italy | 4.30m | Urban challenges |
| Netherlands | 4.30m | Flat terrain |

### Common EU Truck Heights

- **Standard Euro 6**: 4.00m - 4.15m ✅
- **Mega Truck (Netherlands)**: 4.23m ⚠️
- **Standard Semi-Trailer**: 4.05m - 4.15m ✅
- **Refrigerated Units**: 4.08m - 4.18m ✅

### Enforcement

- 📹 Automatic cameras at bridges
- 🚔 Traffic police frequent checks
- 💰 Fines: €200 - €5,000+ (per country)
- 🔒 Vehicle impounding possible
- ⏱️ Delays significant on blocked routes

### Tips for EU Routes

1. **High-Risk Areas**
   - Alpine passes (Switzerland, Austria)
   - Historic city centers (Amsterdam, Prague)
   - Narrow valley roads
   - Medieval town centers

2. **Safe Routing**
   - Use truck-specific navigation (TomTom Truck)
   - Plan major routes via motorways
   - Avoid residential areas
   - Check weight restrictions (Germany)

3. **Border Crossings**
   - Brexit: UK separate from EU rules
   - Balkans: Variable enforcement
   - Eastern Europe: Less regulated
   - Always have documents

4. **Best Practices**
   - Set limit to 3.95m for safety margin
   - Check weather (wind + height = risk)
   - Know truck's exact height (including cargo)
   - Plan alternative routes

## 🔧 How to Measure Truck Height

### Method 1: Manufacturer Specs
- Check truck registration
- Look at service manual
- Contact truck dealer
- Usually: **4.0m ± 0.15m**

### Method 2: Physical Measurement
```
1. Find flat ground
2. Measure from ground to highest point
3. Include any roof racks/antennas
4. Account for cargo weight (suspension)
5. Add 10cm safety margin
```

### Method 3: With Cargo
```
Loaded truck = Lower ground clearance
- Empty: 4.20m
- Half loaded: 4.15m
- Fully loaded: 4.10m

Always use loaded height!
```

## 💡 Pro Tips

### 1. Safety Margin
- **Always subtract 30-40cm** from legal max
- Conservative approach saves money
- One accident = years of payments

### 2. Route Planning
- Use truck-specific GPS
- Check bridge databases
- Plan for fuel stops
- Know weight limit separately

### 3. Documentation
- Know exact truck height
- Keep measurement certificate
- Photo evidence of loading
- Insurance proof

### 4. By Region
- **USA**: 4.11m default (safest)
- **Europe**: 4.15m default (good margin)
- **Russia**: 3.90m default (conservative)

### 5. Weather Considerations
- Wind can "add" height (suspension)
- Heavy load = lower suspension
- Snow/ice increase clearance needs
- Summer safer than winter
